DeliveryOps, end to end

Five short views. Read the tabs in order and you have the whole thing. Detail and evidence live in docs/mockups/ia-step-1.5.html; this is the version to approve or push back on.

What DeliveryOps is, in one sentence

One row per process. Every surface is a view of those rows.

That is the whole design. Today the app has five separate read paths into monday_projects, two hand-transcribed literal files, and a report that labels itself "live" while reading neither. The fix is not more pages — it is one owned table and views over it.

Today

Monday is the pen, DeliveryOps is the photocopy

Every write path in the repo points outward. lib/agent/prompts.ts literally tells the agent "FDE assignments are not writable, update Monday." A photocopy goes stale the first time someone edits the original.

After

DeliveryOps is the pen

A process is created, edited and closed here. Slack, Linear, mail and Kognitos propose changes; a human accepts them. Monday is switched off, not mirrored.

The test it has to pass

Nobody reopens Monday

Not "the report renders". If a single person still edits a board because DeliveryOps cannot express something, the migration failed and the data forks. That is why the edit surface came before the schema was finalised.

Two honest problems with this shape

Active work at 30 rows with 6 lanes averages 5 cards a lane, and one lane is empty. Discovery is genuinely 0 — the active phase mix is Testing/UAT 10, Waiting for Customer 7, Development 1, plus 12 in pipeline and 4 on hold. A six-lane board that is mostly air reads as broken when projected. {Likely} Either collapse to four lanes (Pipeline · Building · Validating · Stuck) or let empty lanes hide themselves. I lean to four lanes, because your milestone taxonomy has five values and the board does not need to mirror it.

7 of the 30 have no recoverable milestone. Monday's Current Phase is one status column, so rows set to "Waiting for Customer" had their milestone overwritten. Import writes phase = null and those 7 need a human pass. They will land in the Blocked lane on day one with nothing behind them.

Your Monday morning, and the team's Friday

The design is only right if these two journeys are short. Everything else is decoration.

Journey A

An FDE updates one process after a call

1
Opens the customer, or the board
Both work. From Customers → Norco → Processes, or from Work → Active. Same drawer either way, so there is nothing to learn twice.
2
Clicks the card
Drawer opens. Everything editable in one place — state, dates, owners, blockers, value. No Save button; each field saves as it changes.
3
Sets phase to UAT
Drawer asks for the date that lane requires. Card moves to the Validating lane. Freshness stamp updates.
4
Done. Never opened Monday.
That is the whole test.
Roughly 15 to 25 of these a month, measured from the archive. That is the entire write load the system has to carry.
Journey B

The team reviews on Friday, and the report writes itself

1
Project Work → Active
30 cards, lanes, big type, no chrome. This is why cards are read-only — an editable grid is unreadable at 3 metres.
2
Suggestion strip at the top
"Linear LIN-482 closed on Norco Warranty." Accept or dismiss live in the meeting. Conflicts show both values, so nobody has to guess which is current.
3
Walk the lanes, open what needs it
Stale cards are visibly stale. Blocked cards with no reason are visibly wrong. Editing happens in the meeting, not after it.
4
Reports reads the same rows
Weekly update and all-hands generate from what was just reviewed. No transcription step, which is what v2-allhands-weeks.ts is today — 1,246 lines of hand-typed literals behind a UI labelled "live".
Where this breaks. {Likely} If the report keeps being hand-assembled after the review, the board becomes ceremony and rots the way the Monday board did — median row age 174 days. The board earns its place only if skipping it visibly degrades the deck you have to present.

Journey C — what the agent is for, once the record is owned

Worth stating because the agent gets more useful the moment DeliveryOps owns the data rather than mirroring it. Today its prompt says FDE assignments are not writable and tells you to update Monday.

"Set Norco Warranty to UAT"
Writes it, stamps provenance as agent, logs an event. Faster than the drawer for a single known change.
"What's stuck and on whom?"
Reads blocked_on across active work. Currently impossible — the field does not exist and Monday blends it into phase.
"Draft the weekly update"
Same rows as the report. The agent and the report cannot disagree, because there is one source.
