My recommendation, and what it survived

Option B now, Option A immediately after cutover. {Likely} The reason is step 1.9 in your own checklist: "verify report numbers match row-for-row before cutover" is a hard gate, and it is far cheaper to verify against an unchanged IA. Doing the merge and the migration in one diff means a number that moves could be either the new data model or the new loader, and you will not know which.

The counter-case, which I think loses: the deduplication in Option B is the expensive half, and doing it without deleting the surfaces means you build loadPortfolio() and then still keep three call sites. That is real waste. It loses because the waste is bounded and one-time, while a botched cutover verification is not.

Either way, three things happen regardless and should be done first: the process drawer (panel 3), migration 0021 (panel 2), and deleting /api/monday/item-updates, which is public and unauthenticated today while holding the server's Monday token.

Saves per field, optimistically. No Save button.

Six mechanics worth agreeing on now

  • Per-field save, optimistic, no Save button. Today's InlineEdit awaits the POST then calls router.refresh() and reads the value back from props, so the new value only appears after a server round trip. In a 20-field drawer that is 20 full-page refreshes. This needs local state.
  • Nothing is greyed out for permissions. Whole team, attribution best-effort — so updated_by from the Auth0 session, no per-field locks.
  • Derived fields are visually distinct and unclickable — dashed border, muted. TTV, run counts, computed value. Prevents the Monday habit of hand-editing a formula column.
  • "Mark reviewed" is separate from editing. Confirming a row is still accurate is the common action, and it is not an edit. Without it, an unchanged row can never leave the needs-review queue.
  • Value shows nothing rather than a modelled number when minutes-saved is unset or the Kognitos link is missing. Per the schema proposal, intended behaviour.
  • History is per-field, not a Monday updates feed. Only 607 of 7,798 archive items have any update at all, so an imported feed would be empty almost everywhere. Per-field history is also the substrate the Slack/Linear/mail suggestions will need — see panel 4.
  • One row is one process for one customer. Confirmed grain, unchanged from the schema proposal. A customer with 4 automations has 4 rows, and the customer 360 groups them.

The board — one surface for editing and for the weekly review

Built to your brief: the working set is a handful of processes, Live and Pipeline sit in separate views, a status change moves the card to another lane, and the same screen has to survive being projected in front of the team. That last constraint is the demanding one — an edit surface optimised for density and a review surface optimised for legibility usually fight. The resolution here is lanes for legibility, drawer for density: nothing on the card is editable, so the card can stay big and quiet.

Default view is 18 rows, not 146. Active work only. Live (71), Pipeline (12) and Closed (45) are sibling views. This is the single biggest reason the board is readable at all — Monday showed you all 146 by default and that is part of why the median row went 174 days without a touch. The four buckets are exact and sum to 146: Live 71, Active 18 (In Progress 14 + On Hold 4), Pipeline 12 (Backlog 10 + Upcoming 2), Closed 45 (Inactive).
Correction to an earlier number in this session. I first put Active at 30 by counting Backlog and Upcoming as in-flight, which double-counts the Pipeline view. Active is 18. Anything you heard from me earlier about "30 in-flight, 24 of them stale" was wrong; the real figure is 18 active, of which 10 are 31–90d stale and 3 are past 90d.

Two things the corrected numbers exposed

The Discovery lane is empty, and that is real. The 18 active rows are Testing/UAT 10, Waiting for Customer 7, Development 1. Nothing is in discovery. Either the team genuinely has no early-stage work in flight, or early-stage work never got logged on the board. Worth knowing which before the board's lane set is fixed, because designing four lanes for a pipeline that only ever occupies two is how you get a screen that looks broken in the weekly review.

Health carries almost no signal on active work. Of the 18: On Track 13, On Hold 4, Off Track 1. Nothing is "at risk" because Monday's Health has no such value in use. Colour-coding cards by health will render 13 green cards, 4 nulls and one red — a legend that earns its space only if the team starts using at-risk honestly. Consider deriving the card border from staleness plus blocked-state instead, which the data actually differentiates.

Import consequence, new. For the 7 rows whose Monday phase is literally "Waiting for Customer", the underlying milestone is unrecoverable — the phase column was overwritten with the waiting state. The schema proposal's mapping says "phase unchanged, blocked_on = customer", but there is no prior phase to keep. Import must leave phase null on those 7 and they need a human pass. That is 7 of your 18 active rows, so it is not a footnote.

Why no drag and drop, given it is a board

{Likely} Dragging sets one field. Every real lane change here also wants a second and third field — moving to UAT wants a date, moving to Blocked wants a reason, moving to Live wants a go-live date, and 7 of your currently-blocked rows have no reason set precisely because Monday let you change status without asking. Opening the drawer on a lane change is the cheap fix for the data quality problem you already have.

Counter-case, stated plainly: drag is faster and feels better in a live meeting, and you asked for fluid. If you want it, the compromise is drag-to-move that opens the drawer pre-filled with the new lifecycle unsaved, so the gesture is fast but the required fields still get asked for.

What the inbound-signal plan costs the schema

This is the part of your note with real schema consequences, so worth settling in 0021 rather than bolting on later. Suggested updates from Slack, Linear or mail cannot write straight into processes — a wrong auto-update is worse than a stale row, because a stale row is at least visibly stale.

Effect on the IA choice in panel 1

Your brief makes this board the weekly-review surface, which is work /reports/weekly does today from a different data path. {Likely} That strengthens Option A, because once the team reviews live on the board, a separately-authored weekly page becomes a second version of the same truth — the exact failure mode the hand-transcribed v2-allhands-weeks.ts literals already represent.

I still recommend B then A, unchanged. The verification gate at step 1.9 is the reason and it has not moved: you need the report numbers to match row-for-row across the cutover, and you cannot attribute a discrepancy if the IA changed in the same diff. Build the board and the drawer now inside the existing nav, prove the numbers, then collapse the pages.
